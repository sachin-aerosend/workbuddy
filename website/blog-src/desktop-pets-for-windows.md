Remember when a little character could live on your desktop? Desktop pets never really went away, and lately they're having a moment. They make a computer feel a bit less like a spreadsheet and a bit more like *yours*.

Here's a tour of well-known options, what makes each one fun, and what to watch out for.

## What makes a good desktop pet?

Before the list, a few things worth checking for any pet you install:

- **Does it stay out of your way?** Some pets are built to be chaotic (that's the joke). Others stick to the edges.
- **Can you pause or hide it quickly?** You'll want this before a meeting.
- **Is it light on resources?** A pet shouldn't slow down your work apps.
- **Where does it come from?** Download from the official page or a trusted store, not random mirrors.

## Shimeji-style mascots

"Shimeji" are small characters that climb your windows, sit on the taskbar, fall off the edge of the screen and multiply if you let them. The community-maintained versions (often called Shimeji-ee) support hundreds of fan-made characters.

- **Great for:** customisation. If there's a character you love, someone has probably made it.
- **Keep in mind:** older versions need Java, and quality varies between character packs.

## Desktop Goose

An indie classic: a goose that wanders your screen, honks, tracks mud, and drags your cursor or silly notes around.

- **Great for:** comedy, and pranking a friend's PC (with permission).
- **Keep in mind:** it's intentionally a nuisance, so it's not a pet you keep running during focused work.

## Typing-reaction cats (Bongo Cat and friends)

A cat that taps along as you type became a meme and then an actual app. It sits in a corner and bops its paws with each keystroke.

- **Great for:** a little bit of joy while writing or coding.
- **Keep in mind:** it's a companion, not a productivity tool: it reacts, but doesn't do anything.

## Full virtual-pet simulators

Some desktop pets go further, Tamagotchi-style: you feed them, they have moods, and they grow over time. A few are available free on game stores.

- **Great for:** people who want a pet to look after.
- **Keep in mind:** needs and notifications can become one more thing asking for your attention.

## Old-school cursor chasers

The classic "Neko" cat that chases your mouse pointer has been ported to pretty much every operating system. Simple and nostalgic.

## A word of caution about the 90s

If you remember BonziBuddy, the purple gorilla, you'll remember it also bundled adware. The lesson still stands: a cute character is no reason to skip checking where software comes from.

## How to choose

- Want **chaos and laughs**? Desktop Goose.
- Want **your favourite character**? A Shimeji mascot.
- Want **a companion while typing**? A typing cat.
- Want **a pet that also keeps you focused**? That's the gap WorkBuddy is trying to fill.

Whatever you pick, a tiny friend in the corner of your screen is a surprisingly nice upgrade to a long workday.
