If your tab bar looks like a row of tiny icons you can't identify, you're not alone. Tabs pile up because closing one feels like losing something. Automating the cleanup takes that decision off your plate.

Here are the options, from Chrome's built-in tools to extensions that actually close tabs for you.

## First: freeze vs close

There are two different things "automatic" can mean:

- **Freezing (discarding):** the tab stays in your tab bar but stops using memory until you click it. Great for performance, does nothing for clutter.
- **Closing:** the tab is actually gone (usually with a way to get it back). This is what fixes the "I can't find anything" feeling.

## Chrome's built-in Memory Saver

Chrome can automatically free memory from tabs you're not using.

1. Open `chrome://settings/performance`.
2. Turn on **Memory Saver**.
3. Optionally add sites that should never be frozen (like a music player or your CRM).

This makes Chrome faster, but your tab bar will look just as busy.

## Tab groups: tidy without closing

Right-click a tab → **Add tab to new group**. Name it ("Proposal", "Research", "Later") and collapse the group when you're not using it. Collapsed groups take up one small chip on the tab bar.

Groups are perfect for tabs you genuinely need this week, just not right now.

## Extensions that close tabs for you

If you want tabs to actually disappear, extensions can do it:

- **Auto-closers** (such as Tab Wrangler) close tabs that haven't been touched for a set time and keep a list so you can reopen them.
- **Tab parkers** (such as OneTab) collapse all your tabs into a single list page in one click.

Look for three things: a configurable time limit, a way to protect pinned tabs, and an easy "reopen" list.

> Pin the tabs you use all day (email, calendar, your main tool). Most auto-close tools, and your own brain, will leave pinned tabs alone.

## Asking instead of auto-closing

Fully automatic closing can feel scary ("what if I needed that?"). A middle ground is a tool that **asks** about a tab you haven't touched in a while and lets you say yes, no or later. You stay in control, but the decision is made for you in two seconds instead of never.

That's how WorkBuddy handles it. When a tab has been untouched for an hour, the cat shows a small thought bubble: *close "LinkedIn | Feed"? untouched for 3 h*. Tap **yes** and it's gone, with an undo if you change your mind. Pinned tabs, tabs playing audio and work sites you mark as safe are never suggested.

:::cta

## A simple setup that works

1. Turn on **Memory Saver** for speed.
2. **Pin** your all-day tabs.
3. Put this week's projects in **tab groups**.
4. Let a tool close, or ask about, everything else after an hour or so.

Most people find they never go looking for the tabs that got closed. That's the real lesson: most of those tabs were already gone; we just hadn't admitted it yet.
