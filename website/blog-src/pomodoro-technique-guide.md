The Pomodoro Technique is one of the simplest productivity methods there is: work in short, timed bursts with small breaks in between. It was created by Francesco Cirillo in the late 1980s, named after the tomato-shaped kitchen timer he used as a student (*pomodoro* is Italian for tomato).

Here's how it works, why it helps, and how to keep doing it.

## How the Pomodoro Technique works

1. Pick one task.
2. Set a timer for **25 minutes** and work only on that task.
3. When the timer rings, take a **5-minute break**.
4. After four rounds, take a longer break of **15–30 minutes**.

That's the whole method. Each 25-minute block is one "pomodoro".

## Why it helps

- **Starting is easier.** "Work on the proposal" is vague and heavy. "Work on it for 25 minutes" is small enough to begin.
- **It protects you from yourself.** When the urge to check Instagram hits at minute 12, you have an answer: after the timer.
- **Breaks are built in.** You rest *before* you're exhausted, so the afternoon doesn't collapse.
- **You learn how long things take.** "That was three pomodoros" is surprisingly useful information for planning.

## How to stick with it

Most people try Pomodoro, love it for two days, then drift. These tweaks help:

### Make the break a real break
Scrolling your phone during a 5-minute break usually turns into 20. Stand up, stretch, get water, look out of a window. Save the feed for a longer break, if at all.

### Adjust the length
25 minutes is a starting point, not a rule. Writing or coding might suit 45–50 minutes with a 10-minute break. Admin tasks might suit 15. Keep the rhythm, change the numbers.

### Keep a "later" list
When a random thought hits mid-pomodoro ("I should reply to that email"), write it on a list and get back to work. You'll deal with it on your break.

### Don't restart the whole day after an interruption
If a call interrupts you, don't write off the afternoon. Start a fresh timer when you're back.

> The timer isn't a boss. It's a promise to yourself that this block is for one thing only.

## Which timer should you use?

Anything works: your phone, a kitchen timer, the **Clock app** built into Windows 11 (its *Focus sessions* are Pomodoro-style), or a browser tab. Pick the one that is visible but not distracting. A phone timer can pull you straight into your notifications.

## Try it today

Pick the task you've been avoiding, set 25 minutes, and only do that. When the timer rings, stand up. You'll be surprised how much one pomodoro gets done, and how much easier the second one is to start.
