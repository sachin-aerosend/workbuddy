Too Many Tabs Open? A 10-Minute Reset That Actually Sticks

By Sachin Sahani · 19 September 2026 · 2 min read

Fifty tabs, no idea which one you need? Here's a quick, guilt-free tab reset and a few habits to stop the pile from growing back.

In this post
  1. Why tab overload hurts
  2. The 10-minute tab reset
  3. Habits that keep it from growing back
  4. The short version

Tabs pile up for a simple reason: closing a tab feels like making a decision, and leaving it open doesn't. Fifty tiny "I'll get back to it" decisions later, your browser is slow and you can't find the tab you actually need.

Here's a 10-minute reset, and a few habits to keep it from happening again.

Why tab overload hurts

  • It's slow. Every open tab uses some memory, especially busy web apps.
  • It's noisy. Each tab is a small reminder of something unfinished.
  • It costs time. Hunting through tiny icons for the right tab, many times a day, adds up.

The 10-minute tab reset

Set a timer for 10 minutes and go through your tabs once, left to right. Each tab gets one of four outcomes:

  1. Close it if you're done with it, or you'd find it again easily with a search.
  2. Pin it if you use it all day (email, calendar, your main work tool).
  3. Group it if it belongs to an active project this week. Right-click → Add tab to new group, name it, and collapse the group.
  4. Save it for later if you genuinely want to read it: add it to your reading list or bookmarks, then close it.

Don't overthink any single tab. If you hesitate for more than a few seconds, it's probably a "close".

When in doubt, close it. Chrome keeps recently closed tabs under History (Ctrl+H), and Ctrl+Shift+T reopens the last one you closed.

Habits that keep it from growing back

One project, one window (or one group)

When you start something new, open its tabs in a fresh window or tab group. When the project is done, close the whole window. One decision instead of fifteen.

Close as you go

Finished with a tab? Close it right away. It's a tiny habit, but it's the one that matters most.

Do a Friday sweep

Once a week, run the 10-minute reset again. It gets faster every time.

Let something ask for you

The hardest part of tab cleanup is deciding. A tool that notices a tab you haven't touched in an hour and simply asks "close this?" turns a decision you'd avoid into a two-second yes or no.

The short version

  • Close, pin, group, or save-for-later: every tab gets one of four outcomes.
  • Work in one window or group per project.
  • Close as you go, and sweep once a week.

Your browser will be faster, and so will you.
